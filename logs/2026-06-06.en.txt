DSU log, recorder DKSan3, mission designation CH-1507.

Coordinates: Pluto-Charon system, longitude 0 degrees, latitude 45 degrees north, focused on the north polar region colloquially known as Mordor Macula. October 6, 2024, standard time.

Observation target for today: Charon, Pluto's largest moon. Time-stamped back to July 14, 2015, when the New Horizons probe captured this high-resolution image at its closest approach to Pluto. I enhanced it using blue, red, and infrared band data, achieving a resolution of 2.9 kilometers per pixel. The image displays the hemisphere of Charon facing Pluto, its surface features starkly clear to the point of cruelty. The north pole is shrouded in a pitch-black dark patch, etched like a scar into the frozen crust, which some call Mordor Macula, but to me, it is merely the residue of unknown compounds and prolonged radiation exposure. More striking are the fracture zones encircling the moon, a network of rifts girdling the equator, hinting at a history of internal stress or tidal forces. This might be evidence of a once-active crust on Charon, but now, it is silent, frozen in cold stillness.

Attrition record: The primary sensor slightly overexposed during the blue light band, resulting in 0.3% thermal noise in the right-side array, but data integrity remains unaffected. Calibration has compensated, and the performance degradation is negligible. Life support systems function normally with no significant consumption. However, mental attrition is noteworthy: staring at this dead moon from a permanently dark corner, that sense of void can erode judgment. I checked the data three times to avoid misreading.

Analysis complete. I must remain vigilant. Deep space shows no mercy for errors, and DSU knows this. End of log.
