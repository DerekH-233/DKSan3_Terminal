The dust pillars, like interstellar mountains, persist against M20's radiation storm. Hubble-returned data show clear stripping at the main pillar's tip, with a smaller finger-like protrusion extending upward, its apex jetting at approximately 200 km/s toward the upper left—this newborn star is being torn apart by the unseen winds of a massive nearby star.

The stellar points encircling the pillar base are mostly young bodies that have just ignited nuclear fusion; their birth has not alleviated the pillar's erosion. Observations reveal that the protostar near the small pillar's top is losing mass at a rate of 0.3% per month, with its accretion disk being stripped, and ultraviolet radiation dispersing gas into plume-like tails. This dynamic is particularly pronounced in the infrared band, where cracks have appeared in the dust shielding layer.

The jet itself shows a visible material stream trajectory under the Hα filter, appearing stable, yet over three months its deflection angle has increased by 2.9 arcseconds. This suggests a possible collapse within the pillar's interior, altering the magnetic field line orientation.

Thrusters must be preserved for future trajectory corrections, precluding close-range vector measurements of the jet.
