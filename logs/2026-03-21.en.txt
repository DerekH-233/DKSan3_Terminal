Galaxies NGC 1300 and NGC 1297 in the river lie at the southern edge of Eridanus, both members of the Eridanus cluster, with distances exceeding 70 million light-years from the reference plane. This session focuses on comparative morphology and structural analysis.

NGC 1300 displays a typical barred spiral structure, approximately 100,000 light-years in diameter, with its face oriented toward the observation point. The central bar structure is clearly defined, spiral arms show significant extension, and dense star-forming regions exist at the arm termini. Based on spectroscopic redshift data, its core exhibits radiation peaks consistent with a supermassive black hole, comparable to the structural model of the Milky Way.

Elliptical galaxy NGC 1297 is slightly more distant, diffuse in morphology, with uniform surface brightness. Preliminary scans indicate its stellar population is predominantly old stars, lacking notable dust lanes. Interaction signs with NGC 1300 are weak, but gravitational lensing models suggest perturbations in the surrounding dark matter distribution.
