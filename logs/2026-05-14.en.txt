Coordinates: RA 00h 48m 26s | Dec +85° 16′ 36″
Observation Target: NGC 188
Status: Complete, minor equipment attrition

NGC 188, a designation derived from Dreyer's 1888 New General Catalogue, yet its true age far predates that catalog. As one of the oldest known open clusters in the Milky Way, its position near the celestial pole renders it nearly perpetually visible. I locked onto it via DSU's deep-space array, at a distance of approximately 6000 light-years. The stellar density at the cluster's core is anomalously high, with most member stars already in the red giant or white dwarf phase—this directly exposes its ancient nature, with an estimated age exceeding 5 billion years.

During observation, the sensor array encountered faint radiation interference from the cluster's periphery, suspected to be residual dust reflecting after the collapse of some old stars. Calibration time increased, resulting in minor hot spots on the optical components' surface—attrition estimated at 0.7%, requiring three more routine scans before refurbishment. Additionally, the coordinate correction system drifted slightly due to polar lock, necessitating reinitialization.

Log concluded. The antiquity of NGC 188 is unsettling; it reminds us that seemingly tranquil regions of the cosmos harbor death and rebirth far more ancient than any civilization.
