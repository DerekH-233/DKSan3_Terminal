**Observation Summary:**
Target is the second-stage rocket exhaust plume from a SpaceX Falcon 9 launch occurring 52 minutes before dawn. Observation conditions met the prerequisites for the "jellyfish phenomenon" formation: ground observation point remained in nighttime shadow, while the high-altitude exhaust plume (primarily composed of water vapor and carbon dioxide) had already captured the rising sunlight. This contrast in light and shadow caused the plume to appear as a luminous cloud-like structure extending upward from the horizon, its visual form appearing to sink due to perspective effects, while actually corresponding to the rocket's continuous ascent phase.

**Phenomenon Analysis:**
This phenomenon is an optical byproduct of rocket launches under specific temporal and spatial conditions (pre-dawn/post-sunset), not an astrophysical event. The plume caused brief perturbations to local upper-atmospheric composition and left behind a diffusing water-ice cloud, potentially producing minor scattered light pollution for subsequent low-luminosity astronomical observations lasting approximately 15-30 minutes.
