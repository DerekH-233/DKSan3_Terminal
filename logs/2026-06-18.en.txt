Log Number: DKSan3-2187
Coordinates: Toward the galactic center, 0.4 degrees lower right from the field center, RA 17h46m15s, Dec -29°01'30". Target: Supernova remnant candidate, presumed to correspond to a core-collapse event around 300 AD.

Synthesized data from the near-Earth joint observation station has been unpacked. The optical channel is provided by PanSTARRS, with background stars marked in red, green, and blue, showing clear structure. MeerKAT radio imagery captures a massive red gas cloud, reading as a star-forming region Sagittarius C spanning about 50 light-years. The X-ray channel, from Chandra and XMM-Newton, displays a blue diffuse halo, partially overlapping the edge of the radio cloud.

Preliminary assessment identifies this blue clump as residual thermal radiation from the supernova remnant. However, a spatial offset exists between the radio and X-ray signals, suggesting the shock front may have interacted with dusty molecular clouds. The remnant's boundary is irregular, making it nearly impossible to define a symmetric structure precisely.

Equipment attrition record: PanSTARRS spectrograph No. 4 showed a signal-to-noise ratio decline mid-scan, suspected to be mirror coating degradation. Two antenna units in the MeerKAT array had data delays of about 0.7 seconds, which did not affect key measurements. Chandra's solar wind particle barrier readings are elevated but still acceptable.

Continue monitoring this region. Data stream is stable. If the shock expansion rate matches expectations, the next imaging window will determine precise distance and age parameters. Observation complete.
