Observation Summary:
The target region has entered the sunlit zone. Sinus Iridum has a diameter of approximately 250 km and is the residual structure of an ancient impact crater, now flooded by basaltic lava, forming a dark, smooth surface. Its arcuate boundary is composed of the Jura Mountains, remnants of the original crater rim. The highest elevation point at Promontorium Laplace rises nearly 3000 m above the bay floor, casting a pronounced shadow under low-angle illumination and clearly delineating the bay's outline.

The term "bay" carries a romanticized misjudgment by early astronomers. The actual environment is a vacuum, devoid of water bodies, with terrain shaped by impact and volcanic processes. This naming convention persists to this day, constituting a form of historical inertia.
