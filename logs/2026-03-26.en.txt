**Observation Log DKSan3-218**
**Coordinates:** GW218-07-14T09:26:35Z | Virgo direction, approximately 130 million light-years from the edge of our local supercluster
**Target:** Black hole-neutron star merger event record No. 218

**Record:**
The gravitational wave signal from this merger event lasted approximately 2 minutes with clear characteristics. The waveform shows a 4.2 solar mass black hole and a 1.8 solar mass neutron star spiraling inward. In the final merger phase, the signal exhibited a distinct cutoff, consistent with the model of the neutron star being disrupted and swallowed by the black hole's tidal forces. The signal frequency surged rapidly from 200 Hz to the kilohertz range within the last 0.1 seconds, forming a typical "chirp" tail.

**Attrition Report:**
During detection, the signal-to-noise ratio experienced a loss of approximately 7% due to perturbations from gravitational lensing effects in the interstellar medium. One node of the VIRGO array suffered a temporary decline in data integrity due to enhanced solar wind particle flux, which was subsequently compensated through cross-validation with KAGRA data. No accompanying electromagnetic radiation was observed for this event, supporting the "dark merger" hypothesis.

**Conclusion:**
This event further validates merger dynamics under extreme mass ratios. The neutron star material completely dissipated outside the event horizon, with no detectable jet formation. Data has been archived in the LIGO-VIRGO-KAGRA joint database.
