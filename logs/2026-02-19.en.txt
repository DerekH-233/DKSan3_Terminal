Date stamp current
Observation coordinates IC 2574, located in the direction of Ursa Major, member of the M81 group of galaxies, at a distance of approximately 12 million light-years.
Observation target Coddington Nebula, i.e., dwarf irregular galaxy IC 2574.

Routine observation report. The target galaxy exhibits an irregular morphology, lacking grand spiral structure, and its aesthetic value is often underestimated. However, spectroscopic analysis confirms the presence of intense star-forming activity. Notable red hydrogen regions are visible within the galaxy, clear evidence of ionized hydrogen from newborn stars. The star formation mechanism is similar to that of spiral galaxies: stellar winds and supernova explosions perturb the interstellar medium, triggering subsequent stellar births. This galaxy has a diameter of approximately fifty thousand light-years, a faint yet thought-provoking isolated island universe.

System attrition. Prolonged exposures to capture its low surface brightness features have led to a 12% increase in cooling system energy consumption. The imaging sensor has shown slight thermal noise due to continuous monitoring of hydrogen-alpha radiation, and calibration protocols have been initiated.

Personal note. Despite its irregular form, the violent stellar birth it hosts is fundamentally no different from that in more celebrated spiral galaxies. This reminds us that glory does not belong solely to those with symmetrical structures. End of observation.
