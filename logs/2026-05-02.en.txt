Mission Log - DKSan3 - Deep Space Joint Standard Recording Unit Designation Θ-7

Titan. Saturn's sixth moon, its surface shrouded under nitrogen-based thick haze, nearly impenetrable to visible light. I have opted for infrared band scanning, positioning the array at 1.2 astronomical units above Titan's orbit. Coordinates: L5 Lagrange point of the Saturn system, offset by 0.03 parsecs, with a relative inclination of 18.4 degrees to Saturn's ring plane. The instrument receives a vast amount of information, yet distortion is not negligible.

Thermal imaging panels reveal a complex river network of liquid methane lakes on the surface, but infrared scattering losses remain significant, with suspended hydrocarbon particles in the upper atmosphere swallowing much of the signal. Among the six tiled panels, the left-wing detector has lost 15% of its sensitive elements due to solar flare interference, while the right-wing thermal sensor shows a 22% calibration drift under continuous exposure. The clearest infrared false-color image suggests a possible thermal anomaly beneath the ice layer—but the haze conceals further details.

Observer attrition: Visual system has sustained minor burns from prolonged direct viewing of infrared return data, within acceptable limits. Direct connection is not recommended; switch to delayed processing. Titan's enigmas will be more deeply unraveled at the next orbital rendezvous. End.
