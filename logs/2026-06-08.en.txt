Observation coordinates: Fornax region, inclination 142.7 degrees relative to the solar ecliptic plane, distance approximately 4.3 astronomical units from Earth.

Comet Panstars R3 has completed its perihelion rush and is now entering its decay phase. Solar irradiation on the nucleus has dropped sharply, with surface temperature falling from -50 degrees Celsius during high activity to below -120 degrees Celsius. Gas and dust jets have significantly weakened; observational data shows its coma diameter contracted by 60% within 72 hours, and albedo dropped from 0.18 to 0.04, nearing a bare-nucleus state. The tail structure is loose, with the ion tail length shrinking to less than 20,000 kilometers, no longer extending to the tens of millions of kilometers as previously. Mass loss rate has decreased to 0.3 kilograms per second, now entering a quasi-dormant phase.

Based on gravitational slingshot model calculations, this comet will return to the inner solar system in approximately 632 years. It will then be designated C/2657 R3, should the DSU archives still exist. Its orbital period indicates it is not a long-period visitor to the Oort Cloud but has been captured into a medium-period orbit due to gravitational perturbations from Saturn or Jupiter. This comet's departure is remarkably quiet—no explosive fragmentation, no signs of disintegration. It chooses to vanish silently into interstellar deep space, calm and precise, much like the posture we observers ought to maintain. End of record.
