Log Entry DKSan3-2688
Observation Coordinates: Cygnus, RAFGL 2688
System Status: Optical and infrared sensor array attrition approximately 2.7%, attributed to sustained focus on high-contrast dust structures.

Target resolved. Egg-shaped nebula, terminal stage of a solar-type star. Composite imaging in visible and infrared data confirms structural analogy: a shrouded, hot core—the "yolk"—illuminating the surrounding "white" shell of gas and dust. Central lobe structures and ring formations are recent ejecta, with dust density sufficient to fully obscure direct line-of-sight to the core.

Key observation point lies in the beams. They are not direct radiation from the core, but rather the result of core light escaping through channels etched by high-velocity polar jets into older ejecta. This explains the directionality of the beams. The structure is dynamic, recording intermittent mass ejections during the star's death throes.

Sensory perturbation note: Observing a star's disintegration is like watching an egg slowly crack open. It answers the question of "what happens if you open the Sun" at the cost of its own destruction. Astronomers are still attempting to disentangle its precise mechanisms. Observation concluded.
