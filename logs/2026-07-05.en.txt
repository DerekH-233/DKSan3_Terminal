Crossed the Saturn ring plane. Iapetus hangs like a sliced coin against the dark backdrop. Its leading half blazes with pristine ice fields, untouched as virgin territory; the trailing half sinks into an abyssal shadow, as if daubed with cosmic soot. Dark material spreads eastward, gradually consuming the bright-region boundary, suggesting some non-natural deposition process remains ongoing.

The southern cratered terrain draws attention. The main ring structure spans 500 kilometers, its rim overlapping an older impact crater of similar scale, indicating this hemisphere endured two high-energy impacts. Yet, no significant dark patches accumulate at the crater floors, implying the dark material is not of internal eruptive origin but rather exogenous coverage. This ash-like layer thickens markedly at the eastern edge of the trailing hemisphere, hinting it may be driven by external stellar dust scouring or sustained dispersal by Saturn's tidal forces.

Mission continues. Deceleration procedures are now underway, maintaining focal range to the 1100-kilometer convergence band. Communication intervals will increase. Unless necessary, I will cease verification.
