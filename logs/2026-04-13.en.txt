**Deep-Space Observation Log**
**Observer:** DKSan3
**Date:** Standard Chronology 2347-284
**Target Coordinates:** SMC NGC 602 (J2000: RA 01h 29m 32.13s | Dec -73° 33' 38.8"); associated background radiation zone "Deep-Space Beyond" (pointing to diffuse interstellar medium region at SMC periphery)

**Observation Summary:**
Successfully located young star cluster NGC 602 at the outer edge of the Small Magellanic Cloud. Its age is approximately five million years, still enveloped within its natal nebula. Hubble data confirm that high-energy radiation and shockwaves from massive central cluster stars have significantly eroded surrounding dust structures, forming characteristic ridge-like and swept-back morphologies. Star formation activity is progressing outward from the cluster center.

"Deep-Space Beyond" refers to the profound interstellar space in the background of NGC 602. Observations reveal faint, unassociated diffuse radiation in this region, possibly originating from more distant or older star formation remnants. Its signal intensity is approximately 18.7% lower than predicted model values.

**Attrition Report:**
- Mirror calibration has lost an additional 3.2% sensitivity due to interstellar dust scattering in the SMC region.
- To penetrate target-area dust, signal-to-noise ratio in the blue end of the spectrum has decreased, requiring subsequent compensation processing.
- No hardware damage. Remaining continuous observation time is sufficient.

**Note:**
The cloud is like an oyster, the stars like pearls. But one must gaze beyond—there lies only the diffusion of nothingness, and fading echoes. Star birth is local and clamorous; deep space itself is a silent consumption.

**End of Log.**
