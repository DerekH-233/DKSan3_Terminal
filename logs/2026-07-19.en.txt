Observation Coordinates: N 38°15'42", W 113°10'28", Utah Desert, altitude 1960 meters.

Target: Disc-shaped wreckage, surface with gray metallic luster, diameter approximately 1.8 meters, debris scattered within a 15-meter radius. Impact crater depth 0.6 meters, azimuth angle 273 degrees. Preliminary assessment indicates it is not an extraterrestrial object, but the sample return capsule of the Genesis probe launched in 2001.

Attrition Report: Outer shell severely deformed, with third-degree penetrating cracks covering 45% of the surface; primary heat shield has lost 60%, with the remainder heavily carbonized. Internal sample container partially fragmented, with an estimated 83% loss of solar wind particle samples. Avionics systems completely nonfunctional, no biological signals detected.

Cause Analysis: The parachute system failed to deploy as planned, resulting in a hard landing at speeds exceeding 300 km/h. Cabin structural integrity was lost, but partial remnants of the core sample module remain.

Long-Term Impact: Failure of this mission will lead to a shortage of solar wind isotope data, delaying solar physics research by approximately 12 years. Deep Space Joint will secure the site for 1024 standard hours to prevent external contamination from interfering with surviving samples.
